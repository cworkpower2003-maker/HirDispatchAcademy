Dispatch desk walkthrough

One load. A chain of decisions.

Every load a dispatcher books passes through the same six stages. Follow one illustrative load from Dallas to Atlanta: scroll through the stages, or select any stage to see what happens at the desk and why it matters.

Stage 1 of 6 · Related module: Truck Dispatching

Review the load opportunity

The dispatcher starts with the truck: where it is, what trailer it pulls and when the driver is free. Then they search load boards for freight that fits.

What the dispatcher does

  • Check the truck's location, trailer type and available date
  • Search load boards for freight near the truck
  • Shortlist the loads that match the equipment and timing

Why it matters

An empty truck earns nothing, but the wrong load can cost more than waiting. Matching the load to the truck and driver first saves time on every call that follows.

Stage 2 of 6 · Related modules: Truck Dispatching, Safety & Compliance

Evaluate the rate and route

Before calling anyone, the dispatcher works out whether the load is worth taking: the rate per mile, the empty miles to reach pickup, the driver's available hours and the delivery window.

What the dispatcher does

  • Work out the rate per mile, including empty miles
  • Check the driver's available hours against the time windows
  • Think about where the truck ends up for its next load

Why it matters

A high rate can hide a poor load. If the route does not fit the driving-hour rules, or leaves the truck far from its next freight, the carrier loses time and money.

Stage 3 of 6 · Related modules: Truck Dispatching, Freight Brokerage

Communicate with the broker

The dispatcher calls or emails the broker who posted the load, confirms the details and negotiates the rate. Clear, polite and professional English is a large part of the job.

What the dispatcher does

  • Call with the load reference and truck details ready
  • Counter the rate with reasons, not just a number
  • Agree extras, such as detention pay, before booking

Why it matters

Brokers work with many carriers. A dispatcher who communicates clearly and keeps their word is easier to trust, and that trust shapes every future booking.

Stage 4 of 6 · Related modules: Freight Brokerage, Billing & Invoicing

Confirm the load and documentation

Once the rate is agreed, the broker sends a rate confirmation. The dispatcher reads it line by line, checks it matches the call, then sends the pickup details to the driver.

What the dispatcher does

  • Read the rate confirmation line by line
  • Match the rate, times and references to what was agreed
  • Send pickup details and reference numbers to the driver

Why it matters

The rate confirmation is the written agreement. A mistake here, such as a wrong rate or missing detention terms, is hard to fix once the truck is moving.

Stage 5 of 6 · Related module: Truck Dispatching

Coordinate pickup and delivery

While the truck is on the road, the dispatcher tracks its progress, records check-ins at pickup and delivery, and keeps the broker and driver updated.

What the dispatcher does

  • Track check-ins at pickup and delivery
  • Send status updates on time
  • Spot delays early and tell everyone involved

Why it matters

Brokers expect to hear from the dispatcher before they have to ask. An early warning about a delay protects the carrier's reputation far better than an excuse afterwards.

Stage 6 of 6 · Related module: Billing & Invoicing

Follow up and complete the workflow

After delivery, the dispatcher collects the signed proof of delivery, sends the invoice with the right documents attached, and follows up until the carrier is paid.

What the dispatcher does

  • Collect the signed proof of delivery (POD)
  • Invoice with the rate confirmation, bill of lading and POD attached
  • Follow up payment and note what was learned on the lane

Why it matters

A load is only finished when the carrier is paid. Missing or incorrect paperwork can delay that payment, so the last step deserves the same care as the first.

    What is truck dispatching?

    A truck dispatcher finds freight for carriers and keeps their trucks moving. The work includes searching load boards, negotiating rates with brokers, booking loads, planning routes and staying in touch with drivers until each delivery is complete. Dispatch work for US and Canadian carriers is done from a desk, using phone, email and software. The dispatch walkthrough shows one example load from start to finish, and our guide What is US truck dispatching? explains the role in more detail.

    Who can join the course?

    The course is designed for beginners who want to work in the North American trucking and logistics industry, including students, graduates, job seekers and career changers. Contact us to confirm eligibility for the next batch.

    Do I need previous experience?

    No. Training starts with the basics of the trucking industry and builds step by step, from beginner to professional dispatcher. Comfort with spoken English and basic computer use will help you in this field. Our guide Skills a beginner truck dispatcher needs explains which skills matter most.

    What topics does the course cover?

    The Complete Truck Dispatch & Logistics Training Program is one course with four modules: Truck Dispatching, Freight Brokerage, Billing & Invoicing, and Safety & Compliance. Every student learns all four. See the course for example topics in each module, and ask our team for the confirmed syllabus for your batch. Our guide Truck dispatcher vs freight broker explains how the dispatching and brokerage sides fit together.

    Are the four modules separate courses?

    No. HIR Dispatch Academy offers one course. The four modules are parts of that course, not separate courses or plans, so you do not choose or join them one by one. Online and offline batches are two ways to take the same complete course.

    What is the difference between online and offline batches?

    Both formats teach the same complete course, with all four modules. Offline batches meet in our classroom on Narwana Road, near HDFC Bank, Patran. Online batches let you join remotely, without travelling to Patran. Class timings, practical work and mentor access are arranged for each format, so ask our team how they work for the batch you are considering.
