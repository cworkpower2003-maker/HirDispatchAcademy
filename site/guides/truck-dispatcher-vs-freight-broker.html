Truck dispatcher vs freight broker: what is the difference?

HIR Dispatch Academy · Published 26 October 2026

In short

A truck dispatcher works on behalf of a carrier to keep its trucks loaded and moving. A freight broker works between shippers and carriers: it takes a shipper's load and finds a carrier to move it. In the US, a broker needs its own FMCSA broker authority and financial security, while a dispatcher acting only for the carrier usually works under the carrier's authority.

This guide describes how the two roles generally work in US trucking. The section near the end explains how HIR Dispatch Academy's course covers both.

How the two meet on every load

On a typical brokered load the broker posts the shipper's freight, the dispatcher finds it for their carrier and calls or emails the broker, and the two negotiate the rate. Once they agree, the broker sends a rate confirmation to the carrier, and the dispatcher checks it before the driver heads to pickup. During the trip the dispatcher sends updates and the broker passes them to the shipper.

Where the line can blur

The difference matters legally. Arranging transport for compensation on behalf of shippers is brokering under US rules, and doing it without broker authority can lead to penalties. In 2023 the FMCSA published guidance on when dispatch services and other intermediaries count as brokers. Anyone setting up an independent dispatch business should read the current FMCSA guidance or take qualified advice first.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Licensing amounts and rules can change, so check the current requirements with the FMCSA.

Why dispatchers benefit from understanding brokerage

  • Negotiation goes better when you understand what the broker needs: a reliable carrier, on-time updates and clean paperwork.
  • Rate confirmations, carrier setup and payment terms all come from the broker's side, so knowing that process avoids mistakes.
  • Brokerage is a separate career path in its own right, and some people work on both sides over time.
